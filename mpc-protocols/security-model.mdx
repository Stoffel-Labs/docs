Use this page when your application has concrete privacy, Byzantine-fault, or availability requirements. Do not treat a backend's configured threshold as one end-to-end guarantee: **preprocessing and online execution protect different assets, depend on different participants, and fail differently.**

## Read the phase boundary first

Preprocessing creates correlated secret material before application values are computed. Online execution consumes that material for input, arithmetic, and output.

| Phase | What is at risk? | What a failure means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preprocessing | Random shares, multiplication triples, random bits, and fixed-point masks | The run can stall, produce unusable material, or—if invalid material is accepted—undermine later correctness or privacy. |
| Online | Application inputs, intermediate values, and authorized outputs | The computation can stall, reveal more than intended, or return an incorrect result. |

An online phase cannot repair bad preprocessing. Conversely, unavailable preprocessing does not immediately stop an online run that already holds enough valid, unused material. It stops the run when that reserve is exhausted.

## Backend-specific parameters

`n` is the number of computing parties. `t` is the maximum number of corrupted computing parties. Input and output clients are not included in `n`.

| Backend | Protocol topology | Reconstruction model | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HoneyBadgerMPC | `n >= 4t+1`, with at least five parties | Online degree-`t` openings normally need `2t+1` matching shares and may need up to `3t+1` received shares to correct `t` errors. Degree-`2t` preprocessing can require `4t+1`. | More parties for the same `t`, but robust private field and fixed-point arithmetic. |
| AVSS | `n >= 3t+1`, with at least four parties | A value can be interpolated from `t+1` verified shares that agree on the same Feldman commitments. Protocol progress still relies on the `3t+1` topology. | Fewer parties and curve-compatible commitments, but group-operation costs and public, non-hiding commitments. |

| Fault threshold | HoneyBadgerMPC parties | AVSS parties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `t=1` | 5 | 4 |
| `t=2` | 9 | 7 |
| `t=3` | 13 | 10 |

A degree-`t` sharing being reconstructible from `t+1` correct shares is not by itself a Byzantine-fault guarantee. Unverified shares can be wrong, while robust reconstruction needs additional distinct, authenticated evaluations.[9]

<Note>
Some Stoffel SDK and off-chain client releases apply a conservative shared `4t+1` validator to both backends. If that layer rejects an AVSS `3t+1` topology, the SDK is imposing a stricter deployment constraint than the AVSS protocol. Check the configuration surface used by your release when planning the actual node count.
</Note>

## HoneyBadgerMPC threat models

HoneyBadgerMPC deliberately uses **optimistic preprocessing** followed by a more robust online phase.[1] The two phases therefore have different availability guarantees even with the same parties and corruption threshold.

### Preprocessing threat model

| Dimension | Guarantee and assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Protected assets | Random and double shares, multiplication triples, random bits, zero shares, and fixed-point masks. No application input is present yet. |
| Threshold | `n >= 4t+1`. Preprocessing handles both degree-`t` and degree-`2t` sharings, with operation-specific reconstruction quorums. |
| Adversary | Analyze a **static, active** adversary controlling at most `t` parties. Adaptive, mobile, and proactive corruption security are not provided. |
| Safety and privacy | Completed material is intended to remain private and suitable for Byzantine-robust online arithmetic, provided all checks complete, randomness is cryptographic, channels are authenticated and confidential where required, and material is never reused. Delays do not justify weaker quorums or unchecked material. |
| Liveness | **Not asynchronously live against all allowed faults.** A required party can withhold a message and prevent a preprocessing batch from completing. Async APIs and timeouts do not turn this into guaranteed termination. |
| Interaction and cost | Several dealing, checking, reconstruction, and broadcast stages. Local work includes polynomial generation, matrix transforms, degree-`t` and degree-`2t` reconstruction, and fixed-point mask generation. |
| Operational consequence | Generate and monitor a reserve before accepting work. A stalled replenishment job need not invalidate material already completed, but the online phase must stop safely when valid material runs out. |

### Online threat model

| Dimension | Guarantee and assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Protected assets | Client inputs and secret intermediate values; only explicitly authorized outputs should be reconstructed. |
| Threshold | `n >= 4t+1`. Arithmetic uses degree-`t` shares and robust openings; multiplication consumes one valid, unused triple per product. |
| Adversary | Analyze a **static, active** adversary controlling at most `t` parties. Robust opening addresses malformed or inconsistent shares. Adaptive state exposure and secure-erasure guarantees are not provided. |
| Safety and privacy | Conditional on correct, fresh preprocessing, the online field-arithmetic path keeps inputs private up to the threshold and checks robust openings. Fixed-point operations additionally depend on their range and statistical-mask assumptions; their source construction uses a semi-honest model.[8] |
| Liveness | **Conditionally asynchronously live.** With enough valid preprocessed material, sufficient honest participation, and eventual honest-to-honest delivery, robust online operations can progress. Material exhaustion, finite operation limits, absent clients, or a permanent partition can still stop the run. |
| Interaction and cost | Linear operations are local. Multiplication opens masked differences in parallel. Input, output, multiplication, and fixed-point truncation create separate dependency stages. |
| Failure boundary | A robust online opening cannot detect every defect in material accepted during preprocessing. Preprocessing validity is an explicit precondition, not something the online threshold re-establishes. |

## AVSS threat models

The AVSS backend uses encrypted share delivery and Feldman commitments. Feldman verification checks that a received scalar share matches a public commitment polynomial; it does not make the commitment hiding and does not prove every higher-level relation a protocol needs.[10]

### Preprocessing threat model

| Dimension | Guarantee and assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Protected assets | Random Feldman shares and multiplication triples intended for later scalar arithmetic. |
| Threshold | `n >= 3t+1`. Shares have degree `t`; random-share generation uses all dealers, while triple generation selects `2t+1` product dealers. |
| Adversary | A **static, passive** adversary is the strongest model under which the integrated triple-generation arithmetic can currently be treated as correct without another product-relation check. Active share-consistency checks exist, but they do not establish end-to-end active security. Adaptive corruption security is not provided. |
| Safety and privacy | Encrypted delivery provides computational confidentiality in transit. Feldman commitments are binding but public and non-hiding. More importantly, commitment consistency does not prove that a triple's committed output equals the product of its committed inputs. A malicious selected dealer can consistently share the wrong product. |
| Liveness | **Not asynchronously live against all allowed faults.** Random-share generation waits for all dealers, and triple generation waits for every dealer in a fixed `2t+1` set. One required dealer can withhold and stall preprocessing. |
| Interaction and cost | Concurrent encrypted dealings are followed by local mixing. Triple generation adds product resharing. Work includes polynomial evaluation, encryption, group commitments and verification, matrix mixing, and scalar multiplication. |
| Operational consequence | Do not treat generated triples as malicious-secure without an additional validated product-relation mechanism. Pre-generating more material addresses capacity, not this correctness limitation. |

### Online threat model

| Dimension | Guarantee and assumption |
| --- | --- |
| Protected assets | Scalar inputs, intermediate shares, commitment-bearing arithmetic state, and output shares. Public commitments are part of the transcript, not protected data. |
| Threshold | `n >= 3t+1`. Reconstruction needs `t+1` verified shares with the same commitments; multiplication opens two masked differences and consumes one triple. |
| Adversary | Online opening rejects shares that do not match expected commitments, providing a useful defense against **active** malformed-share behavior. End-to-end multiplication correctness nevertheless falls back to the preprocessing model because online execution assumes a valid triple. Adaptive and proactive corruption security are not provided. |
| Safety and privacy | Linear arithmetic and openings are conditionally consistent when parties share the same expected commitments. Multiplication is correct only if the consumed triple satisfies its product relation. Public commitments permit offline guessing of low-entropy values, so AVSS is not a hiding boundary for ordinary application data. |
| Liveness | **Conditionally asynchronously live.** It requires valid preloaded material, enough honest responses, eventual delivery, and required client participation. Automatic replenishment crosses back into the weaker preprocessing liveness model. |
| Interaction and cost | Linear operations are local. Batched multiplication opens two masked differences in one broadcast stage, followed by local field and group work. Input broadcasts a masked correction; output sends verified shares to its client. |
| Failure boundary | Online commitment checks can reject malformed openings, but cannot certify that preprocessing produced `c=ab`. A consistent online transcript can therefore still compute the wrong result from a bad triple. |

## Compare the phase transitions

| Question | HoneyBadgerMPC | AVSS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can one faulty required party stall preprocessing? | Yes | Yes |
| Can preloaded material let online work continue during a preprocessing outage? | Yes, until the reserve is exhausted | Yes, until the reserve is exhausted |
| Does online execution actively validate received openings? | Yes, through robust reconstruction | Yes, against expected Feldman commitments |
| Does that online validation establish the validity of preprocessing? | No; correct and fresh material remains an assumption | No; in particular, it does not establish the triple product relation |
| Is low-entropy application data hidden from the protocol transcript? | Yes, subject to the stated threshold and deployment assumptions | No general claim: public Feldman commitments permit candidate testing |
| Is adaptive corruption covered? | No | No |

## Asynchronous safety is not liveness

**Asynchronous safety** means message delay alone cannot make honest parties accept incompatible or incorrect results or cross the stated privacy boundary. A safe protocol may still stop.

**Asynchronous liveness** means honest parties eventually complete despite up to `t` faulty parties, assuming eventual delivery between honest parties and fair scheduling. It does not cover a permanent partition, missing required client input, or exhausted preprocessing.

Randomized asynchronous agreement can have an expected-round bound without a fixed completion deadline. FLP explains why asynchronous consensus cannot promise deterministic termination in every execution with even one fault.[14]

## Shared deployment assumptions

- Party-to-party and client-to-party traffic needs authenticated identities and confidential channels. Reliable broadcast provides agreement, not encryption.
- Random generators must be cryptographically secure.
- Masks, random shares, and triples must be consumed once, including across cancellation, retries, crashes, and restored state.
- `t` is the total corrupted-party budget, not one allowance for Byzantine parties plus another for crashed parties.
- Neither backend supplies differential privacy. Authorized outputs and repeated queries can still disclose application information.
- Constant-time execution, side-channel resistance, forward secrecy, and formal universal composability are separate deployment requirements.

## Choosing between the backends

Choose **HoneyBadgerMPC** for private arithmetic over application values when you can provision and monitor preprocessing reserves. Its online path is the stronger fit for active Byzantine behavior, but its topology uses more parties and its fixed-point protocols carry additional range and masking assumptions.

Choose **AVSS** when the application deliberately needs public commitments or curve-compatible scalar shares. It supports a better protocol-level fault ratio, but those commitments are not hiding and the current triple path does not establish malicious multiplication correctness.

If you require adaptive corruption security, guaranteed completion of preprocessing under every allowed fault, or a formally verified malicious-security composition across both phases, neither backend alone establishes that requirement.
